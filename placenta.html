Placenta

The placenta is a transient yet indispensable organ—the critical interface between a mother and her developing fetus. Its vascular architecture governs nutrient exchange, waste removal, and oxygen delivery, linking maternal and fetal physiology.

Abnormal development of the placental chorionic vasculature is associated with a range of complications, including preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, and fetal growth restriction. These conditions not only affect maternal health but can also influence long-term outcomes for the child, such as metabolic and neurological disorders.

We model the development of the placental chorionic plate vasculature using a reduced-order mechanistic framework that relates growth dynamics to the balance between placental expansion and vessel formation. MicroCT scans of healthy, full-term placentas provide quantitative constraints on model parameters, enabling comparison with pathological cases and offering insight into how deviations from normal growth may underlie disease-linked morphologies.
